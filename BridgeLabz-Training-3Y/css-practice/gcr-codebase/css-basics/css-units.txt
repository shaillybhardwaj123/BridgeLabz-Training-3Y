Here are the short, clear answers:

---

### **Q1: PPI vs DPI**
* **PPI (Pixels Per Inch):** The density of **digital pixels** on a screen (monitors, phones). Higher PPI means a sharper screen.
* **DPI (Dots Per Inch):** The density of physical **ink dots** printed on paper by a printer.

---

### **Q2: Physical Pixel vs Logical Pixel**
* **Physical Pixel:** The actual hardware light dot on a screen. 
* **Logical Pixel (CSS Pixel):** A unit of measurement in code.
  * *Example:* On high-resolution screens (like Retina), **1 Logical Pixel** in your CSS might map to **4 Physical Pixels** ($2 \times 2$) to keep layout sizes readable instead of tiny.

---

### **Q3: Value of 1 CSS Pixel in Inch and Cm**
By standard CSS definition ($96\text{px} = 1\text{ inch}$):
* **In Inches:** $1\text{px} = \frac{1}{96}\text{ inch} \approx 0.0104\text{ inches}$
* **In Centimeters:** $1\text{px} \approx 0.0264\text{ cm}$ (or $0.264\text{ mm}$)

---

### **Q4: Why is `em` tricky/avoided for Padding?**
You *can* apply `em` to padding, but it behaves differently:
* **Relative to itself:** When used on `padding`, `1em` is relative to the **element’s own font-size**, not the parent's.
* **The Problem:** If you change the element's `font-size`, its padding will automatically change too. This makes spacing inconsistent across different elements.
* **Solution:** Use **`rem`** instead. `rem` is relative to the root (`<html>` font size), so it keeps your spacing consistent everywhere.
